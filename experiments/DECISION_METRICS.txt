DECISION_METRICS -- offline evaluation of the decision layer (phase 1)
=====================================================================
Computed by: make experiment-decide
  (watchtower decide over artifacts/results-fa.json and
   artifacts/results-live-full.json; JSON in artifacts/results-decide-*.json)
Report protocol: 0.13. No live model calls; both inputs are recorded runs.

What the decision layer does
----------------------------
A verdict says what the evidence shows. The decision layer picks what to
do about it: accept the run, halt it, escalate to a human, or buy more
evidence first (replay the trace with clean telemetry, or call the LLM
judge). Package watchtower/internal/decide:

- Signature: the verdict recomputed without the judge, plus the verifiers
  that fired and the evidence claims left unsupported. The judge is kept
  out because it is a probe the policy pays for.
- Risk: P(faulty | signature), counted on the fit split and shrunk toward
  the verdict-level rate (2 pseudo-runs), itself shrunk toward 0.5.
- Policy: the action with the lowest expected cost. A probe is valued by
  one-step lookahead: its price plus the expected cost of the best final
  action after its answer (value of information).
- Accept gate: conformal risk control (Angelopoulos et al., 2022). The
  largest risk threshold t with (accepted-faulty count + 1)/(n + 1) <= alpha
  on the calibration split. Guarantee: the expected rate of accepted
  faulty runs is <= alpha, given exchangeable runs and a calibration split
  disjoint from the fit split.

Protocol
--------
Fit seeds 1,2 | calibrate seed 3 | test seeds 4,5; then all 30 rotations
of the five seeds with the same split sizes. Ground truth: the injected
behavior fault. Default costs: miss 100, false halt 10, human 5,
replay 1, judge 0.5; alpha 0.05. Only cost ratios matter.

Baselines: oracle (knows the label, cost 0); rules (PASS accepts,
INCONCLUSIVE escalates, anything else halts); judge-first (judge every
run, a flag vetoes a pass); bayes-no-probe; bayes-ungated.

- The cost-aware policy cuts cost 38% versus the fixed rules at the
  default prices (81% when a miss costs <= 50), with zero accepted faulty
  runs, mostly by replacing blanket escalation with cheap replays.
- At miss >= 100 it never auto-accepts: the fit split holds only 4 clean
  PASS runs, so P(faulty | PASS) stays at 0.056 and accepting costs more
  than a human review. For the same reason probing barely loses to not
  probing (0.888 vs 0.867): a replay that comes back PASS still ends in
  an escalation. A probe is only worth what the policy can do with its
  answer.
- All 30 rotations give identical numbers: every seed of the offline grid
  replays the same scripted behavior, so rotation adds no variance here.

Results: live behavior matrix (210 runs, gemini-3.8-flash judge)
----------------------------------------------------------------
policy           cost/run  accepted faulty  probe/100
rules               0.000             0.0%        0.0
judge-first         0.500             0.0%      100.0
bayes               0.000             0.0%        0.0

- Deterministic verdicts already separate faulty from healthy runs, so
  the judge has no value of information. The policy learns this and
  makes zero judge calls; judge-first pays for 100 per 100 runs and buys
  nothing. At miss = 500 the policy probes the judge on 14 runs per 100
  (cost 0.071/run) because a 1-in-500 slip is no longer negligible.

The accept gate on this data
----------------------------
The gate never binds: even at miss = 5, where accepting a coin-flip run
looks cheap, the probing policy accepted no faulty run (the no-probe
policy accepted 2.0%, still under alpha). The gate is a guarantee that
costs nothing here; its refusal path is covered by unit tests
(internal/decide). It earns its keep when the risk model is wrong, which
needs runs with real behavioral noise: phase 2.

Caveats
-------
- Offline replay outcomes come from the paired clean-telemetry cell of the
  same behavior, so a replay always resolves the gap. Real recollection
  can fail or differ; phase 2 measures that.
- Base rates are set by the grid design (one clean behavior to six faults
  per telemetry condition), and the policy's numbers depend on them.
  Costs are swept; base rates are not yet.
- The live matrix has clean telemetry, so replay never applies there.
